Answer Me

By Zoey :3 • August 2026

“Matron.”

Keyleth silently cursed herself for the way her voice shook on the word. She had to be confident, she had to be steady. This was too important.

It had been weeks since Vox Machina had beaten the Whispered One. Vecna had put up quite a fight, but by the skin of their teeth they’d survived and killed him, hopefully for good this time. As the Raven Queen’s champion, Keyleth had been entrusted with his remains, and as the Voice of the Tempest she’d known just what to do with them. Good luck getting your eye back from under that mountain, asshole.

Still, it wasn’t quite the happy ending she’d hoped for. When he’d first emerged from the ruins of Thar Amphala, Vecna’s first act had been to eliminate his greatest threat: the Matron’s champion. That little bolt of necrotic magic had flown straight for Keyleth’s heart, faster than she could ever hope to react. There was no stopping it, she knew. This would be the end.

Until Vax got in the way. Of course he had; that was Vox Machina’s specialty after all. The love of her life had somehow slipped the bonds of a god and moved quicker than death itself, just so he could turn to dust in front of her eyes. He’d traded his own life for hers, the idiot, and Keyleth’s grief at the thought had almost brought Whitestone down on top of them.

And unlike usual, he’d stayed dead. So now Keyleth needed to fix it.

“Matron,” she said again. She could feel the strength in her voice this time. That was good, she’d need it. She’d tried speaking to the Duskmaven before of course – every day since it happened, in fact – and she’d never heard anything back. She’d have to make this time different.

Keyleth placed her hands flat against the ground and curled her fingers into the dirt, rooting herself. She let her consciousness flow down her arms and into the earth, connecting to the trees, the beasts, the very soil itself, looking for her targets. When she found them, she moved in, her mind filling their bodies. She felt feathers, talons, a million eyes looking down from a million trees, rooftops, the open sky – and she could see through them all.

Matron.

The tempest’s voice echoed from the beaks of every raven on the continent. Their Queen wouldn’t ignore her this time.

Answer me.

The sunlight that had been filtering through Keyleth’s eyelids – her own eyelids – vanished. Slowly, she opened them, and saw exactly what she’d been hoping for. An infinite, black void, and the empty, porcelain mask of the Matron of Ravens.

“Champion,” said the Matron, her voice as calm and level as always, but Keyleth had no interest in pleasantries.

“Where is he?”

“Of whom do you speak?”

“Who the fuck do you think!?” The rage in her own voice surprised her, as did the tears welling in her eyes. She was falling apart already, shit, she’d been hoping to last longer than this.

Oh well, in for a copper. “We did everything right,” Keyleth continued, letting the grief pour through into her voice – there was no point trying to hide it now. “We found the diamond, Pike said the magic words, we even killed the godsdamned Whispered One for you! We did every part of the ritual exactly right and nothing. Happened. Why?”

Silence. Though the Matron’s mask stayed as blank as ever, Keyleth couldn’t help but imagine expressions in it. Disappointment. Contempt. Boredom.

WHY!?

The question rang through the void as a cacophony. The birds, Keyleth was still linked to them, and their voices had added to hers as a wall of grief and rage and desperation. And Keyleth might have been imagining it, but as that wall hit the Matron, she almost seemed to flinch.

Keyleth decided to let the silence sit for a moment. She fought the urge to wipe her face, even as the tears ran down it.

Finally, the Matron answered. “He cannot be returned,” she said simply, flatly. “There is no deal you can make. He followed the thread of his fate to its conclusion, and now his time is over.”

How can it be over? Keyleth wanted to scream. How can his fate end there, and not with me? She thought back to Vax’s so-called “end”. How Vecna had offered him a choice, to kneel or watch Keyleth die, and then almost killed Keyleth anyway. An empty choice, the illusion of control in the face of a god who’d already decided what he wanted.

Suddenly, the Matron seemed familiar. Keyleth flicked a finger – a quick cantrip to clear the tears from her vision – and fixed defiant eyes on the mask in front of her. “You say that like it’s a fact. Like it’s fixed. But it isn’t, is it? It’s you.” She was on dangerous ground, here, but what else was new. “You’re the Matron of Death. You could bring him back right now if you wanted, you’re just choosing not to. So, again, wh-argh!”

The force of the Raven Queen wresting the druid’s mind out of her followers was enough to knock Keyleth to the ground. “It is my choice, Champion,” the Matron’s voice seemed to echo around the void, “and I have made it.”

Keyleth blinked, and looked up at the Matron’s mask. It was above her now, and huge, and Keyleth felt like she knew what it was to be a bug trapped under a glass, looking up at her captor’s face.

But this, too, was familiar. The Matron was making herself look bigger, hoping to intimidate her: a threat display. Ordinary, mortal, people do that too. So do peacocks.

The Voice of the Tempest wasn’t afraid of peacocks. “Why is it your choice?” She stood, locking eyes again with the mask’s empty features. “What gives you the right to decide who lives and who dies?”

“I am a god.”

“Well maybe you shouldn’t be! Maybe…” Keyleth averted her eyes, lowered her voice. Her sorrow and frustration compelled her to finish the sentence, but some survival instinct at least allowed her not to look the Matron in the eye when she did. “Maybe the Whispered One was right about you.”
